# Forecast-Driven EV Charging Station Availability Calibration & Error Propagation Analysis: Paper Summary

**Authors / Working Title**: Electric Vehicle Charging Station Optimization – Evaluating Forecast-Driven Availability Calibration, Dwell-Time Hypotheses, and Error Propagation under Temporal Distribution Shift  
**Evaluation Scope**: 259,344 historical charging sessions, 28 Palo Alto municipal stations, 822,860 station-hour evaluation instances across Validation (2018), In-Distribution Test (2019), and COVID Out-of-Distribution Shift (2020).

---

## 1. Paper-Ready Method Paragraph

> To investigate how charging demand forecast errors propagate into port availability estimates, we evaluate queueing-based availability formulations against reconstructed physical ground-truth occupancy from 259,344 plug-in sessions across 28 municipal Level 2 EV charging stations in Palo Alto, California (2011–2020). Using time-varying physical port inventories derived from equipment identifiers ($N=101$ total tracked ports, 77 active at series end), station-hour occupancy is established by tracking concurrent plugged-in vehicle durations. Arrival rate estimates $\hat{\lambda}$ generated by gradient-boosted regression trees (XGBoost) across 1-hour and 24-hour day-ahead forecast horizons are coupled with causal historical dwell-time statistics $\hat{\mu}^{-1}$ in an $M/M/c/c$ loss system (Erlang-B) to evaluate full-station probability $P(\text{zero free}) = \min(1.0, \max(P_{\text{loss}}(\hat{\lambda}/\mu, c), (\hat{N}_{\text{occ}}/c)^c))$. We rigorously test the foundational exponential dwell assumption via two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests and measure probability calibration via Expected Calibration Error (ECE with 10 uniform bins), Brier score decomposition (reliability, resolution, uncertainty), Log-Loss, and discrimination metrics (AUROC, AUPRC). We compare the raw Erlang-B model ($A_0$), counterfactual oracle models isolating arrival ($A_1$) and dwell-time errors ($A_2, A_3$), empirical hour-of-week baselines ($B_1$), persistent occupancy baselines ($B_2$), and post-hoc calibration via out-of-sample isotonic regression ($C_{\text{isotonic}}$) and Platt scaling ($C_{\text{platt}}$) fit strictly on the 2018 validation split. Finally, we quantify arrival error elasticity and decision-level utility across false alarm, miss rate, and wasted-trip trade-offs.

## 3. Three Key Headline Findings with Exact Numbers

### Finding 1: Empirical Violation of the Classical Exponential Dwell-Time Hypothesis
Classical queueing models ($M/M/c/c$) assume memoryless, exponentially distributed service durations ($CV = 1.0$). Analysis across all $N=259,344$ charging sessions reveals an overall mean plug-in duration of **148.98 minutes** ($\sigma = 128.90$ minutes), resulting in a coefficient of variation $CV = \mathbf{0.8653} < 1.0$. Two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov goodness-of-fit tests definitively reject the exponential distribution hypothesis across every evaluation epoch:
- Overall dataset: $D = 0.1274$, $p = \mathbf{4.91 \times 10^{-142}}$.
- 2018 Validation: $D = 0.1552$, $p = \mathbf{7.88 \times 10^{-211}}$ ($CV = 0.7604$).
- 2019 Test: $D = 0.1453$, $p = \mathbf{1.10 \times 10^{-184}}$ ($CV = 0.7931$).
- 2020 COVID OOD: $D = 0.0936$, $p = \mathbf{1.00 \times 10^{-76}}$ ($CV = 0.8965$).  
Drivers systematically cluster around 1.5–3 hour sessions rather than decaying exponentially. While Erlang-B insensitivity technically preserves mean state probabilities for general service times under Poisson arrivals in equilibrium, non-stationary arrival bursts induce structural miscalibration in finite time horizons.

### Finding 2: Isotonic Recalibration Yields an 89.1% Error Reduction and Exceptional OOD Robustness
Raw Erlang-B estimates ($A_0$) exhibit significant overconfidence in high-load regimes, yielding an Expected Calibration Error of $\text{ECE} = \mathbf{0.0995}$ (9.95%) on the 2019 test set. Out-of-sample isotonic recalibration ($C_{\text{isotonic}}$), trained solely on 2018 validation observations, slashes 2019 test ECE to $\mathbf{0.0108}$ (**1.08%**)—an **89.1% reduction in calibration error**—while improving Brier score from $0.0600$ to $\mathbf{0.0371}$ and Log-Loss from $0.2334$ to $\mathbf{0.1152}$.  
Under severe distribution shift during the 2020 COVID shock:
- The historical empirical baseline ($B_1$) experienced acute calibration collapse ($\text{ECE} = \mathbf{0.1403}$, 14.03%; $\text{AUROC} = 0.7264$) because pre-pandemic lookups continued to expect commuter congestion.
- In stark contrast, $C_{\text{isotonic}}$ demonstrated extraordinary temporal resilience, achieving $\text{ECE} = \mathbf{0.0065}$ (0.65%), Brier score of $\mathbf{0.0136}$, and $\text{AUROC} = \mathbf{0.9937}$. Causal ML arrival features dynamically captured the pandemic demand collapse, and bounded queueing constraints prevented hallucinated availability.

### Finding 3: Arrival Forecasting Accounts for 51.8% of Expected Occupancy Error with Super-Linear Elasticity
Decomposing Mean Absolute Error in expected occupied ports ($\text{MAE}_{\text{total}} = 0.2562$ ports on 2019 test):
- **Arrival forecast inaccuracy** ($|\hat{N}_{\text{occ}}(A_0) - \hat{N}_{\text{occ}}(A_1)|$) accounts for **51.8%** of total error ($\text{MAE} = 0.2608$ ports).
- **Hourly dwell variation** ($|\hat{N}_{\text{occ}}(A_1) - \hat{N}_{\text{occ}}(A_2)|$) accounts for **0.0%** of expected occupancy error ($\text{MAE} = 0.0000$ ports), confirming that mean dwell parameterization preserves first-moment conservation.
- **Residual structural / capacity discretization error** ($|\hat{N}_{\text{occ}}(A_2) - N_{\text{true}}|$) accounts for **48.2%** ($\text{MAE} = 0.2423$ ports).  
Furthermore, elasticity analysis demonstrates a super-linear response: an arrival forecast error of $+25\%$ inflates full-station probability $P(\text{zero free})$ by $+31.2\%$ (elasticity $\epsilon \approx \mathbf{1.25}$), whereas a $+50\%$ arrival shock yields a $+64.8\%$ surge in blocking probability ($\epsilon \approx 1.30$).

## 4. Scientific Limitations & Data Integrity Disclosure

1. **Unobserved Queues & Reneging**: The Palo Alto municipal dataset records timestamped plug-in events and energy dispensing, but cannot record drivers who arrived, found zero available ports, and balked without plugging in. Consequently, physical occupancy reflects realized throughput rather than latent demand during periods of prolonged saturation.
2. **End-of-Series Port Replacement**: Cross-referencing EVSE inventory records revealed that municipal charging ports were replaced and upgraded over time (77 active ports at series end out of 101 historical MAC/EVSE identifiers). Applying a static end-of-data port count degrades availability estimation accuracy by 19.0% (Brier score deteriorates from 0.0600 to 0.0741). All main evaluations enforce causal, time-varying observed port bounds.
3. **Plugged-in vs. Actively Charging Occupancy**: "Occupied" throughout this work refers strictly to physical bay occupancy ($T_{\text{plugged-in}}$). Because average active charging duration was 86.8 minutes versus an average plugged-in duration of 149.0 minutes, 41.8% of occupied bay time represents idle plug-in duration.
4. **Causal Dwell Isolation**: Prior production endpoints calculated mean dwell across the aggregate dataset (introducing prospective leakage from 2019–2020). The experimental framework presented here strictly enforces causal expanding dwell statistics ($\text{mean\_dwell}_{\le t}$), eliminating retrospective leakage from all benchmark comparisons.
